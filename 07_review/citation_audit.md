# Citation audit: `06_proposal/proposal.md`

**Audit date: 2026-11-02.** Every source below was opened as of 2026-11-02. Scope: all 25 citekeys in the proposal, every sentence that cites them, and the factual statements without citations. This is a report only. No project file other than this one was changed.

**Method.**
- Metadata was checked against Crossref (`api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`, no mailto), OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, RePEc/IDEAS, and the publisher or issuer page or PDF.
- Claims were checked against the full text wherever a public copy opened: publisher, central-bank or author PDF.
- Thai documents were read in the original. Scanned pages were read from the page images.
- PDFs were saved under the session scratchpad (`audit_downloads/<name>/`) and parsed with `python -I`.
- Locators give the PDF page ("PDF p.") and, where it differs, the printed page.

**Verdict scale.**
- **OK**: the source supports the claim.
- **MINOR**: the wording overstates the source or is imprecise.
- **MISMATCH**: the cited source does not support the claim.
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: the source could not be opened.

### Required fixes, most serious first

1. **MISMATCH: `th2020appraisalmr`, §4.1.** The proposal says values "are adjusted mechanically for parcel depth and shape [@th2020appraisalmr]". The Ministerial Regulation contains no depth or shape adjustment.
   - Clause 2 lists parcel shape only as a field to record during the survey. Clause 6 says per-unit values are used to set each parcel's value, without saying how.
   - The depth and shape factors come from the Treasury Department guide *แนวทางการใช้บัญชีราคาประเมินที่ดิน* (PDF p. 3, depth tables pp. 21–22). That guide is hosted only on a local-government site (talad.go.th).
   - Fix: cite the guide as well, with its hosting caveat, or drop "mechanically for parcel depth and shape" from the MR-backed sentence.
2. **MINOR, matters for identification: `bot2011circular1564`, §4.2, and the uncited sentence in §1 reason 2.**
   - The 2011 circular restricts only the **classification forbearance** to debtors classified *pass* or *special mention* (PDF p. 1, item 1). Banks could assist other debtors under the normal rules.
   - Item 2 (PDF p. 2) explicitly extends assistance to debtors affected **indirectly, outside the declared disaster areas**.
   - Q&A item 1 (PDF p. 9) says the BOT set **no cut-off date**; 25 July 2011 is offered only as a practical guide.
   - So "Relief covered only debtors who were performing" and "Eligibility depends on … declared zones and dates" do not hold for 2011. They do hold for the 2024 regime (circular ฝนส.(01) ว.601/2567).
   - Fix: write "Classification forbearance covered only debtors classified pass or special mention", and attribute the zone and date rules to the 2024 circular.
3. **MINOR: novelty claim in §3 "The gap", citing `grundke2018macroeconomic`.**
   - Grundke & Pliszka support the statement that reverse stress tests over macro factors exist.
   - "Not for hazard" sits uneasily with two papers the proposal itself cites:
     - Caloia & Jansen ask "when floods would start impairing financial stability" (IDEAS abstract). The project's own notes, not re-verified here because the DNB PDF returns 403, record that they call their design "in essence, a reverse stress test".
     - Blickle et al. compute the disaster severity that would push a single-county bank to failure.
   - Fix: "Caloia and Jansen (2021) trace capital depletion along a ladder of flood scenarios, and Blickle et al. (2025) extrapolate the disaster severity that would fail a local bank; neither solves formally for the breaking severity S*. Formal inversions exist for macroeconomic factors [@grundke2018macroeconomic]." The ledger's open item (DNB WP 796 not read) still stands.
4. **MINOR: `albertazzi2026physical`, §3 table, "insured settings".** The paper's own literature review says that "only 25% of catastrophe losses in the EU are insured, with coverage rates below 5% in several Member States" (PDF p. 9). Italy is among its four floods. Fix: "advanced-economy setting; corporate loans only".
5. **MINOR: `th2020appraisalmr`, §2 RQ1.**
   - Under the regulation, **provincial committees** set the values, not the Treasury Department.
   - Registered sale prices are one item in a "such as" list of market data (clause 1). The regulation does not say values come "mainly" from them.
   - Fix: "Provincial committees, with the Treasury Department as secretariat, set them by market comparison, using market data such as registered sale prices".
6. **MINOR: `bot2019collateral`, §2.** The absence was confirmed for circular ว.575/2562 only. "No public Bank of Thailand rule" is broader than what was searched; the ledger itself notes that later LTV notifications were not opened. Fix: "The Bank of Thailand's collateral-appraisal guideline makes no reference to them [@bot2019collateral]".
7. **MINOR: `gallagher2017household`, §1.** One Katrina paper cannot carry "the insurance channel that dominates US results". Fix: "the flood-insurance channel that drives household debt outcomes after Hurricane Katrina [@gallagher2017household]".
8. **MINOR: `chitchumnong2025physical`, §3 table.** "Default stays below predictions" leaves out the first quarter. In 2011Q4 actual default was *above* the model, which the authors put down to "ineligible delinquent borrowers" (PDF p. 8). Fix: "After a small rise in 2011Q4, default ran below predictions, which the authors attribute to relief."
9. **MINOR: `caloia2021flood` and `ecb2022climate`, §1 template.** The ECB report supports hazard → collateral → LTV → LGD (PDF p. 44). Neither source opened says PD moves "to a lesser degree"; the Caloia full text could not be opened. Fix: drop "and, to a lesser degree, PD", or cite a page that states it.
10. **MINOR: `pei2017devil` and `davezies2017regression`, §4.1.** "Estimators robust to error in the running variable" overstates both papers. Each gives identification only under specific conditions; Davezies & Le Barbanchon need an auxiliary sample in which the true and noisy running variables are both observed. Fix: "draws on identification results for RD with a mismeasured running variable [..], whose assumptions will be assessed for satellite flood edges."
11. **MINOR: `dolk2025assessing`, §1.** "A later study" is inaccurate: the blog is dated 2 June 2025, before the Chitchumnong WP (August 2025). It is also a blog summarising a joint World Bank–BOT analysis. Fix: "A World Bank–Bank of Thailand district-level analysis found…".
12. **MINOR, uncited: §1, "In those economies insurance and public aid absorb much of the loss."** This is contradicted for the EU by the 25% figure quoted in Albertazzi et al. (PDF p. 9). Soften, or cite a source per region.
13. **MINOR, uncited: §4.1, "2 km cells in rural areas and 500 m cells in towns".** `04_analysis/src/mapsheet.py` also handles 1 km (1:2,000) and 250 m (1:500) sheets. Fix: "mostly 2 km cells in rural areas and 500 m cells in towns".
14. **UNVERIFIABLE: `caloia2021flood`, §1.** `dnb.nl` returned HTTP 403 for the WP 730 PDF and the block was not bypassed. Open https://www.dnb.nl/media/st5psfvb/working_paper_no-_730.pdf by hand to confirm the PD/LGD statement and the "in essence, a reverse stress test" quote (project notes cite p. 18 and p. 5).
