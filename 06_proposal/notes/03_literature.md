## Novelty sweep (2026-11-02, `01_literature/strands/S9_official_valuation_sweep.md`)

- Claim (i) narrowed from "first evidence" to "first controlled estimate". Reason: Utami et al. (2021) overlay Semarang's official land-value zone (ZNT) maps for 2014–2019 and find the tidal-flood-prone zone rose least. It is descriptive, chronic tidal flooding, no controls, so it does not do what RQ1 does, but it is evidence on emerging-market official values where land floods. Call ZNT "official land-value zones", not tax assessments (ZNT is BPN's zoning, not the NJOP property-tax base).
- Thailand: no study relating Treasury appraisal values to a flood found (ThaiJO 21 queries, OpenAlex 69, Crossref, web). Semantic Scholar not searched (rate-limited); re-run before submission.
- New fact used in §2: after 2011 the Treasury surveyed flooded areas, found no fall in land prices, and used the late-2011 values unchanged from 1 July 2012, after a six-month flood postponement (MGR Online, 15 June 2012; press report of the Director-General). This is the Treasury's own claim that λ_T was about zero in 2011; RQ1 tests it. Weak point: press, not a Treasury document. The six-month postponement is also a precedent for the round-timing risk in §7.
- Possible additions if space allows (not in the proposal): Davlasheridze, Hou and Miao (2026, JHE), Harvey cut Harris County assessed values about 1.5% (abstract only); Sato and Shiba (2021, PLoS ONE), Japan's official land prices fell in low-lying coastal areas after the 2011 tsunami (abstract only).
