## 1. The "3-year SFI moratorium for debt under THB 500,000" (timeline row 8)

### Headline finding: the 2011 scheme was not targeted at flood victims

The World Bank calls this a flood measure for "flood-hit borrowers". The press report of the Cabinet decision does not support that. The scheme was a general debt-moratorium policy for **small farmers and low-income debtors with repayment problems**, adopted during the floods. The THB 500,000 ceiling was measured at a **pre-flood reference date (30 June 2011)**, and neither eligibility rule mentions flood damage.

### What I found

**Cabinet resolution of 15 Nov 2011 (มติคณะรัฐมนตรี วันที่ 15 พฤศจิกายน 2554).** The scheme was called โครงการพักหนี้เกษตรกรรายย่อยและประชาชนผู้มีรายได้น้อยที่มีหนี้คงค้างต่ำกว่า 5 แสนบาท (Debt moratorium project for small farmers and low-income people with outstanding debt below THB 500,000).

Source: the MGR Online / ASTV Manager Daily report of 15 Nov 2011, 22:41, "ครม.ไฟเขียวพักหนี้ ต่ำกว่า5แสน3ปี" (Cabinet approves 3-year moratorium for debt under 500,000). It reports the announcement by Deputy Finance Minister บุญทรง เตริยาภิรมย์. This is a press report of the resolution, not the resolution text.

| Item | Terms as reported |
|---|---|
| Institutions | Six SFIs: ธ.ก.ส. (BAAC), ธนาคารออมสิน (GSB), ธพว./SME Bank, ธอส. (GHB), ธอท. (Islamic Bank) and บตท. (Secondary Mortgage Corporation, SMC). |
| Group 1: BAAC | BAAC customers who borrowed for occupation or housing **and** were in arrears and/or had restructured debt, **or** were customers in the three southern border provinces (Yala, Pattani, Narathiwat). |
| Group 2: other SFIs | Individual customers of GSB, SME Bank, GHB, Islamic Bank and SMC who borrowed for occupation or housing, whose debt was **NPL and/or restructured**, **and** who were in the personal income-tax system. |
| Debt ceiling | Remaining principal **summed over all contracts at all institutions** of no more than THB 500,000 per person (ยอดต้นเงินกู้คงเหลือรวมทุกสัญญาในทุกสถาบันการเงินรายละไม่เกิน 500,000 บาท). |
| Reference date | Debt status as at **30 June 2011** (ณ วันที่ 30 มิ.ย.2554). |
| Relief | Voluntary moratorium on **both principal and the interest accruing** over the period (พักชำระต้นเงินกู้ และดอกเบี้ยที่เกิดขึ้นในช่วงเวลาดังกล่าว), followed by a new repayment plan. |
| Duration | **3 years, backdated:** 1 Oct 2011 to 30 Sep 2014. |
| Conditions while enrolled | No new borrowing from the SFI granting the moratorium; borrowers could leave early; they received occupational rehabilitation and training. |
| Registration | Opened 15 Nov 2011, with about 3 months for registration and screening. |
| Size estimate | About 775,090 eligible accounts (12.48% of the six SFIs' accounts), with THB 90,502.55 m outstanding (5.76% of their outstanding debt). |
| Flood eligibility | **None stated.** The report links the scheme to the floods only through the surrounding news. In the same story, the Comptroller General's Department paid civil-servant salaries early "หวังเยียวยาช่วงน้ำท่วม" (to ease hardship during the floods). |

**Phase 1 closed on 15 Apr 2012, and the Cabinet extended the scheme on 24 Apr 2012 (มติคณะรัฐมนตรี วันที่ 24 เมษายน 2555).** Source: ThaiPublica, 25 Apr 2012, reporting the Finance Minister's press conference of 24 Apr 2012 (กิตติรัตน์ ณ ระนอง).

The press conference framed the whole scheme as post-flood recovery: "หลังจากประเทศผ่านพ้นภัยพิบัติน้ำท่วมใหญ่ช่วงปลายปี 2554 … จัดโครงการพักหนี้เป็นเวลา 3 ปี" (after the late-2011 floods, the government set up a 3-year moratorium).

- **Phase 1 results:** it ran 15 Nov 2011 to 15 Apr 2012. There were 661,508 authors, of whom 428,384 were approved, covering THB 52,301.87 m of debt.
- **Phase 2 coverage:** borrowers with **Normal (performing) status** at **four** SFIs (GSB, BAAC, SME Bank, Islamic Bank), who had been SFI debtors **before 24 Apr 2012**, with debt of no more than THB 500,000.
- **Phase 2 exclusions:** housing loans, hire-purchase or leasing, salaried-borrower loans, and loans already on special interest rates.
- **Phase 2 relief:** borrowers chose either (1) a principal moratorium plus a 3% a year interest cut, or (2) the 3% cut with normal principal repayment. Either option ran for 3 years, 1 Sep 2012 to 31 Aug 2015. Borrowers who defaulted were removed from the scheme.
- **Phase 2 funding:** MOF compensated the SFIs for 1.5 percentage points of the cut, and the SFIs absorbed the other 1.5. About 3,758,226 borrowers were expected, with THB 459,113.05 m of debt.
- **Movement between phases:** Phase-1 NPL debtors who returned to Normal after restructuring could switch to Phase 2.

**Secondary corroboration.** Isranews (13 Aug 2023) reviews the Cabinet resolutions of 15 Nov 2011, 24 Apr 2012 and 27 Aug 2013, using the resolutions and BAAC annual reports. It says the 15 Nov 2011 scheme covered only "หนี้ที่มีปัญหาการชำระหนี้" (debts with repayment problems). The 27 Aug 2013 resolution extended the THB 500,000 moratorium to **cooperative and farmer-group members**. I read this article through a summarising fetch tool, not in full, so treat these details as [UNVERIFIED].

### Implications for identification note 4

- **Eligibility.** The 2011 rule turns on **pre-flood repayment status** (NPL, restructured or in arrears) and **total principal ≤ THB 500,000 at 30 Jun 2011**, not on flood exposure. Both the threshold and the status were fixed before the floods. That suits an RD at THB 500,000, because debt could not be manipulated in response to the flood, although manipulation in anticipation of the July 2011 election pledge is possible. The treatment, however, is debt relief in general, not flood relief.
- **Do not call row 8 a "flood-hit borrower" moratorium in the proposal** unless the World Bank's source is found. The 2012 Phase 2 has a different threshold date (debtors before 24 Apr 2012) and covered performing borrowers instead.
- **Ceiling definition.** The ceiling sums principal **across all financial institutions**, not only SFIs. Running the RD would need credit-bureau data, not SFI data alone.
- **Lender coverage.** GHB and SMC took part in Phase 1 but not Phase 2.

### Still not found

- The Cabinet resolution text of 15 Nov 2011 and of 24 Apr 2012: resolution.soc.go.th is blocked. The Secretariat document numbers are unknown.
- The SFI implementing announcements: for example a BAAC, GSB or GHB ประกาศ or ระเบียบ for the scheme.
- The cooperative-loan component (27 Aug 2013) from a primary source.
- Any separate **flood-specific** SFI moratorium in Oct–Nov 2011 that the World Bank might be describing. Search results showed only shorter SFI flood measures, for example up-to-12-month suspensions, in undated or later news items, which I did not open. [UNVERIFIED]

## 2. Disaster Insurance Promotion Fund (timeline row 11)

### The Emergency Decree (Royal Gazette text read)

**พระราชกำหนดกองทุนส่งเสริมการประกันภัยพิบัติ พ.ศ. 2555** (Emergency Decree on the Disaster Insurance Promotion Fund, B.E. 2555)
- **Citation:** ราชกิจจานุเบกษา เล่ม 129 ตอนที่ 10 ก, 26 มกราคม 2555, pp. 5–10 (Royal Gazette vol. 129, part 10 Kor, 26 Jan 2012, pp. 5–10).
- **Dates and authority:** given on 26 Jan 2012 (ให้ไว้ ณ วันที่ 26 มกราคม พ.ศ. 2555) under s. 184 of the 2007 Constitution, and countersigned by PM ยิ่งลักษณ์ ชินวัตร. In force from the day after publication (s. 2), i.e. 27 Jan 2012.
- **Read from:** the Internet Archive copy (captured 2022-04-30) of the Gazette's own file, `ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/DATA/PDF/2555/A/010/5.PDF`. The PDF metadata shows it was created on 2012-01-26. The URL was taken from ThaiPublica's 2012 list of the four decrees.

Key provisions:
- **s. 3** defines "ภัยพิบัติ" (disaster) as windstorm, flood, earthquake/landslide (ธรณีพิบัติภัย), and other disasters the Minister announces.
- **s. 4** sets up the fund as a juristic person within the Ministry of Finance. Its purpose is to manage disaster risk by **underwriting insurance and reinsurance, and giving financial assistance to non-life insurers**.
- **s. 7** puts the fund outside the Non-Life Insurance Act and the OIC Act.
- **s. 10–11** leave underwriting, policy types and forms, premium rates and claims payments to criteria **announced by the Fund's Board** (ประกาศคณะกรรมการ).
- **s. 12** lets the fund help insurers with catastrophe claims, in return for insurer contributions at a rate the Board sets.
- **s. 13** lets MOF, with Cabinet approval, borrow in THB or foreign currency for the fund, **up to THB 50,000 m in total**.
- **s. 18** sets up the Board: a Cabinet-appointed chair, the MOF Permanent Secretary, the FPO Director-General, the OIC Secretary-General, and up to four experts.
- **s. 25** lets the Cabinet dissolve the fund on the Minister's proposal when it is no longer needed.
- **Reason note (p. 10):** after the 2011 floods, insurers struggled to manage catastrophe risk, so premiums became very high or cover could not be bought.

**Important correction to row 11.** The decree itself sets **no THB 100,000 cap and no premium rates**. These come from the Board's underwriting guidelines approved by the Cabinet, set out below.

### Coverage terms: Board guidelines approved by the Cabinet on 6 Mar 2012

- **The Cabinet decision.** MGR Online / ASTV Manager Daily of 6 Mar 2012, "ครม.ไฟเขียวกองทุนฯอัตราดบ.ประกันภัยพิบัติ", reports Finance Minister กิตติรัตน์ ณ ระนอง: the Cabinet "วานนี้ (6 มี.ค.)" approved the underwriting guidelines (แนวทางการรับประกันภัยพิบัติ) proposed by the Fund's Board. The Board had met 5 times between 9 Feb and 1 Mar 2012. This is a press report; the Cabinet text was not read.
- **The OIC slide deck.** It is titled "แนวทางการใช้ประโยชน์จากกรมธรรม์ภัยพิบัติ" (How to use the disaster insurance policy). Its PDF metadata says it was created on 2012-06-15, and it is hosted on boi.go.th. Slides 2, 5 and 7–12 confirm the terms below.

| Insured group | Disaster cover limit | Premium a year | Flood claims | Deductible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Homes (บ้านอยู่อาศัย) | Sub-limit of **no more than THB 100,000**. It was added automatically to the "กรมธรรม์ประกันอัคคีภัยและภัยพิบัติสำหรับที่อยู่อาศัย" (residential fire-and-disaster policy). | 0.5% of the cover | Paid by water depth: water on the floor 30% of the sub-limit; 50 cm 50%; 75 cm 75%; 100 cm 100%. MGR gives these as THB 30,000, 50,000, 75,000 and 100,000. | None |
| SMEs (sum insured ≤ THB 50 m, per MGR) | Up to 30% of the sum insured. Sold as a separate "กรมธรรม์ประกันภัยพิบัติ" (disaster insurance policy) that needs an underlying fire or IAR policy. | 1.0% | Actual loss, up to the sub-limit | 5% of the sub-limit |
| Industry (sum insured ≥ THB 50 m, per MGR) | Up to 30% of the sum insured | 1.25% | Actual loss, up to the sub-limit | 5% of the sub-limit |

**Trigger.** A loss counts as a covered "disaster" only in three cases (OIC slide 10, MGR, and the policy wording PDF below):
1. the Cabinet declares a severe disaster situation on the advice of the Ministry of Interior (DDPM);
2. total claims under disaster policies exceed THB 5,000 m from one event within 60 days, with at least 2 claimants;
3. an earthquake of magnitude 7 or more, or wind of 120 km/h or more.

So household payouts for floods were tied to a **Cabinet disaster declaration**. That is a further link to identification note 2 on declared zones.

**Other facts**
- **Board appointment and launch.** OIC slide 5 says the Cabinet appointed the Board on 7 Feb 2012, chaired by พยุงศักดิ์ ชาติสุทธิผล, with Thaire Life/Thai Reinsurance (บมจ. ไทยรับประกันภัยต่อ) contracted to run reinsurance on a quota-share basis (slide 6). The existing timeline (row 11, BOT article) gives the launch of sales as 28 Mar 2012.
- **Business policy wording.** The fund's site (ncif.or.th, Internet Archive copy of `th/pdf/PolicyDisaster.pdf`, created 2012-05-09) has the wording of the business "กรมธรรม์ประกันภัยพิบัติ (Catastrophe Insurance Policy)". Its definitions repeat the three-part trigger. Exclusion 3.8, though the text layer is garbled, appears to exclude property in areas the government designates as **flood-retention or floodway areas** (พื้นที่รับน้ำ / ทางน้ำไหลผ่าน) whose owners get state compensation. Read it from the page image before citing. [UNVERIFIED wording]
- **Dissolution.** An Internet Archive copy of `ncif.or.th/pdf/มติครม.ยุบกองทุน.pdf` ("Cabinet resolution dissolving the fund") is a scanned letter from the Secretariat of the Cabinet to the Finance Minister. It is dated **15 Oct 2015** and reports the Cabinet meeting of **13 Oct 2015**. That meeting approved MOF's proposals under the 27 Jan 2015 revolving-fund reform, including **merging 2 and dissolving 2 revolving funds** under s. 7 of the Revolving Funds Act B.E. 2543 (พระราชบัญญัติให้อำนาจกระทรวงการคลังรวมหรือยุบเลิกทุนหมุนเวียน พ.ศ. 2543). Page 1 does not name the funds. That the Disaster Insurance Promotion Fund was among those dissolved rests only on the file name and its hosting on the fund's own site. [UNVERIFIED] Pages 2–7 were not read.
