# Policy and institutional timeline: flood risk, collateral valuation, classification and provisioning, climate supervision (Thailand)

**Working draft, as of 2026-11-02. For the author to rewrite in their own voice.**

Scope: Thai rules that bear on how floods reach collateral values, credit-risk classification, provisioning and bank capital, plus the relief measures that censor observed default. The machine-readable version is `policy_timeline.csv`, generated from the same rows.

**Sourcing.** Every fact below comes from a public document opened on 2026-11-02: BOT circulars, policies and press releases, BOT web pages and, where noted, the World Bank. "Verified = yes" means the measure was read in an original official document from the issuer (or, for two rows, in a BOT document that cites it, as stated in the row). "Secondary-only" means the row rests on the World Bank–RTG assessment (`worldbank2012thai`), a press report, or a secondary description. Of 26 rows, 18 are verified from primary documents and 8 are secondary-only.

**Reading notes.**
- The 2011 BOT circular ว. 1564/2554 is a scanned PDF. Pages 1–2 (the circular itself) were read from the page images; the two Q&A sheets and the reporting tables were read from the text layer.
- Several World Bank rows from `S5_notes.md` §3 are now replaced or corrected by the original BOT circular: classification forbearance, the credit-card minimum payment and the eligibility definition (rows 2–4). The rest stay secondary-only (rows 6–9).
- One World Bank claim conflicts with the BOT text. The World Bank says restructured flood debtors could leave the NPL list after **one** payment. The BOT Q&A (item 3) keeps the **three**-month rule for upgrading Special Mention debtors to Normal. The one-payment rule may be in another circular that I did not find, so row 6 stays secondary-only and should not be cited as BOT policy.

## Variation usable for identification

Each item lists the rule, the type of variation it creates, the research question it serves, and the main threat. "Interpretation" marks my judgement, not established evidence.

**1. Pre-flood classification status as an eligibility cut-off (2011 and 2024–25). Serves RQ2.**
- ว. 1564/2554 froze classification only for debtors who were **Normal or Special Mention** when the flood hit (row 2). The December 2025 Level-4 moratorium excludes debtors that were already **NPL** on the Cabinet resolution date, 2 Dec 2025 (row 25).
- Flooded borrowers already in arrears therefore faced observed default with no relief, while performing ones had their default censored. This matches the 2011Q4 spike among ineligible delinquent borrowers reported by `chitchumnong2025physical`.
- Use: compare flood elasticities of default and restructuring across the eligibility line, within flooded areas.
- Threat: pre-flood status is not randomly assigned. Defaults close to the line (Special Mention versus Substandard) can be compared using days-past-due at the flood date.

**2. Declared disaster zones and their timing. Serves RQ2; the declaration dates also feed RQ3.**
- 2011: relief was for debtors in DDPM-declared emergency disaster areas *and* for debtors outside them who were indirectly affected. BOT set no cut-off date and suggested 25 Jul 2011 as a guide (rows 2 and 4). The soft-loan decree also targets flood-affected areas (row 10).
- 2024: the template is keyed to the **date each area was declared** a disaster zone or reached yellow, orange or red level, with a 12-month window (row 22). Because declarations differ by province and district, relief windows are **staggered in time and space**.
- 2025: the Level-4 "extremely severe" zone defines a sharp **spatial boundary** for the 12-month moratorium and for the LTV and risk-weight relief on repair loans (row 25).
- Use: a spatial RD or a border DiD at Level-4 zone boundaries, and an event study on declaration dates. Satellite flood extent (Sentinel-1) then separates *flooded* from *declared*. That separation is the key to telling the flood effect from the relief effect.
- Threats:
  - the 2011 rule also covered indirectly affected debtors outside the zones, which blurs the 2011 boundary;
  - declarations may be endogenous to local political pressure;
  - lender discretion (relief "may" be granted) makes eligibility only intention-to-treat.
- Data need: DDPM declaration records by date and area. These are not yet located.

**3. Collateral revaluation timing: the 2012 deferral and the 3-year cycle. Serves RQ1 and RQ2 (LGD).**
- From 20 Jan 2012, collateral and foreclosed property whose revaluation fell due by 30 Jun 2012 could keep the **old (pre-flood) appraisal** for up to one more year, but no later than 31 Dec 2012 (row 5).
- The 2019 policy requires credit-impaired real-estate collateral to be revalued **every three years** and foreclosed property every year. For performing and under-performing collateral, frequency is at the bank's discretion, and AVMs are allowed (row 14).
- *Interpretation:* when flood damage enters book collateral values depends on a pre-determined revaluation due date, not on the flood. With loan-level data, that due date is plausibly exogenous to flood severity and gives staggered timing for measuring the valuation lag and its effect on LGD and provisions. It also implies that stale, pre-flood values persist longest in stage 1–2 loans, which is where H2a expects understatement.
- Threats: banks could revalue early at their discretion; the deferral applied in all areas, which helps exogeneity but lowers first-stage strength.

**4. Lender type and the THB 500,000 total-debt threshold at state banks (2011). Serves RQ2 and RQ3. Secondary-only (press); not flood-conditioned.**
- Per the World Bank, the 3-year SFI moratorium applied to flood-hit borrowers with total debt **under THB 500,000** (row 8). **Corrected 2026-11-02** (press report of the 15 Nov 2011 Cabinet resolution): eligibility was pre-flood status (NPL, restructured or BAAC arrears) and total principal at all institutions ≤ THB 500,000 on 30 Jun 2011, with **no flood condition**. The threshold therefore identifies general debt relief, not flood relief; flood × threshold interactions remain possible but the threshold needs credit-bureau totals across lenders. Manipulation before 30 Jun 2011 (the July 2011 election pledge) would need a density test.
- The Cabinet resolution text itself is still unopened (resolution.soc.go.th behind Cloudflare).

**5. Regime changes in classification and provisioning: TFRS 9 from 1 Jan 2020. Serves RQ2 (staging) and RQ3.**
- สนส. 23/2561 moved classification to performing, under-performing and credit-impaired (stages 1–3) for financial statements beginning on or after 1 Jan 2020 (rows 12–13).
- The 2011 flood happened under the pre-TFRS 9 classification rules; the 2024–25 floods happened under TFRS 9 with a classification freeze.
- *Interpretation:* comparing flood responses across the two regimes speaks to whether ECL staging surfaces flood risk earlier. But this is a single time-series break, confounded with COVID-era relief (see "Could not verify"). It motivates the question more than it identifies an effect.

**6. Fixed calendar of Treasury appraisal rounds. Serves RQ1.**
- Official land appraisal values are reset in rounds: 2023–2026 is in force, and the 2027–2030 round is planned for 1 Jan 2027 (rows 17 and 26; both secondary-only for dates).
- Because the round calendar is set independently of flood events, floods between rounds (2024 north, 2025 south) allow a round-to-round DiD of flooded versus nearby unflooded parcels once the 2027 values are out.
- The BOT collateral policy does **not** reference Treasury values (row 14). So the RQ1 link from official appraisals to bank collateral values must be argued (for example via the reported Treasury–Krungthai–GHB appraisal data-sharing agreement, not verified) or shown with data, not assumed.

**7. Climate-supervision rollout by bank group. Serves RQ3 (calibration and motivation only).**
- Standard Practice in 2023, with BOT assessment from 2024 (row 18).
- A physical-risk climate stress-test pilot with D-SIBs and other "ready" banks (row 21).
- The World Bank flood scenario that BOT asked banks to use (row 24).
- *Interpretation:* the D-SIB versus non-D-SIB split could support a descriptive comparison of disclosed flood stress-testing practice. It is weak for causal work: selection into the pilot is voluntary, and outcomes are not public.
