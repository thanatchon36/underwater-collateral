# Pre-analysis plan, RQ1 2024 design — working draft (not registered)

Draft of 2026-11-02, amended 2026-11-04 (additions dated in the text; all before any post-2023–26 round exists), for the author to edit and register on OSF Registries before the 2027–30 round of official values is published (by law at least 30 days before it takes effect; by 2 December 2026 for a 1 January 2027 start). Registration needs the author's account (CP3). Numbers come from scripts in `04_analysis/src/`; nothing here uses the 2027–30 round.

## 1. Question and hypotheses

- H1a: at the first round after the 2024 floods, values on flooded land fall relative to comparable unflooded land in the same province (confirmatory: treatment coefficient β < 0), less where ex-ante hazard was high (secondary: a negative treatment × height-above-valley-floor coefficient, since low-lying land is the high-hazard land; tested by the wild cluster bootstrap).
- H1b: carry-over (unit value unchanged between rounds) is more frequent on flooded land.

## 2. Units and outcome

- Unit: cadastral map sheet, any scale (round 13 correction: the samples always included all scales; 2024 treated: 120 at 1:4,000, 65 at 1:1,000, 12 at 1:2,000, 1 at 1:500). Controls are not selected by scale; instead the weights match the treated share of rural (1:4,000) sheets within each province and the rural indicator enters the regression (round 13; before it, 13% of controls were rural sheets against 61% of treated sheets: 241 of 1,891 and 120 of 198). Restricting to 1:1,000 and 1:4,000 is a sensitivity. Pseudo land unit: parcels sharing a sheet and a 2023–26 value.
- Primary outcome: change in log value per square wah between the 2023–26 round and the first post-flood round, averaged over pseudo land units present in both, sheets weighted equally. Units are followed by parcel number: a unit's later value is the mean over its parcels still present; parcel numbers new in the later round (subdivisions, mergers) are ignored, and 2023–26 parcels missing from it count as attrition (share of a sheet's 2023–26 parcels), reported by treatment group with the number of sheets that lose every parcel.
- Secondary: carry-over share; change where values moved, with Lee (2009) bounds.
- Weighting (round 12): sheets are weighted equally because values are published, and treatment is measured, by sheet, and the question is about valuation decisions, not land area (committees price land units, mostly road or waterway frontages, which the file does not identify; a pseudo land unit is not a legal land unit, since one land unit can span sheets and its parcels' values differ by depth and shape factors; `04_analysis/treasury_method_notes.md` §1a; corrected 2026-11-03). Sensitivities: weights proportional to sheet area (a 1:1,000 urban sheet is about 0.25 km², a 1:4,000 rural sheet about 4 km²) and to the number of parcels in the 2023–26 round.

## 3. Treatment: two co-primary samples

**Radar sample** (`flood_s1_grd.py`, `rq1_2024_pooled.py`).
- Sentinel-1 GRD, VV, overview level 2 (about 80 m), sigma0 from the sigmaNought LUT, GCP geocoding to 0.001°.
- Windows: north of 11° N 15 Aug–31 Oct; south 15 Nov–31 Dec; seasons 2024, 2025; reference 2023.
- Water: sigma0_VV < −18 dB. Flooded in season Y: water in at least one season-Y scene, water in no 2023 scene, seen in at least two 2023 scenes.
- Usable sheet: valid pixels at least half the bounding box in 2024 and 2025.
- Treated: 2024 share > 25% and 2025 share ≤ 25%. Sensitivities: 10% and 50%.
- Provinces: at least 25 treated sheets (currently Chiang Rai 88, Nong Khai 60, Nakhon Phanom 25, Phayao 25). Sensitivity: at least 20 (adds Nakhon Si Thammarat 23; 221 treated) and at least 30 (Chiang Rai and Nong Khai only; 148 treated) (`rq1_sample_sensitivity.csv`; its MDEs, 0.044 and 0.060, use radar-only controls and are not comparable with the primary 0.047).
- Validation counts: UNOSAT comparison uses sheets radar shows > 25% flooded in 2024 (114 in Chiang Rai–Phayao); the treated sample also requires 2025 ≤ 25% (113).

**UNOSAT sample** (`exposure_2024_north.py`, `rq1_2024_design_power.py`, which writes `rq1_2024_unosat_sample_sheets.csv.gz`; registered code `rq1_round_change.py --event unosat`): Chiang Rai and Phayao (both also in the radar sample, so the two Holm-adjusted tests are positively correlated); treated if the UNOSAT FL20240912THA share > 25%; 1,214 treated in 18 clusters; 579 controls with UNOSAT share 0 and radar 2024 and 2025 shares < 1% (601 hazard-comparable, less 22 radar-wet), 28 clusters (10 holding both). Same weights and regression as the radar sample. Phayao has 17 controls for 378 treated sheets, so its balancing falls back to built-up share and the rural indicator (`rq1_round_change_unosat_balance.csv`; effective controls 104 in all, MDE 0.061). Placebo footprints cannot be drawn (fewer controls than treated sheets), so this sample's primary p-value is the wild cluster bootstrap's; pre-flood standardised differences against the 601 hazard-comparable controls (before the 22 radar-wet sheets leave) −0.04 (value) and 0.04 (height), but not balanced on land cover (cropland 0.57, built-up −0.35; `rq1_2024_balance.csv`), which enters the regression; the registered weights balance all five variables in Chiang Rai and only built-up share and the rural indicator in Phayao.

**Multiplicity and decision rule.** H1a's β is tested in both samples; the two primary p-values (radar: placebo footprints in the draft rule, wild bootstrap under the amendment proposed in §6 on 2026-11-04; UNOSAT: wild bootstrap) are Holm-adjusted (a family of two when every province shares one timing case; with mixed timing the families are set in §9; `rq1_round_change_holm.csv`). H1a is supported if β < 0 with a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05 in at least one sample of the R1 family; with mixed timing, the case-(b) provinces' R2 tests form a second family, Holm-adjusted on their own and reported as a separate confirmatory test of those provinces; a β > 0 with adjusted p below 0.05 is reported as evidence against H1a (values rising on flooded land, e.g. reconstruction), not as support. Everything else is secondary and labelled as such; H1b is descriptive.

## 4. Controls

- Dry in every source (round 12): radar 2024 and 2025 shares < 1% and UNOSAT 2024 share < 1% where UNOSAT maps the sheet; same provinces (scale is handled by the weights, §2). Radar sample at 5 km: 1,891 controls (2,373 hazard-comparable radar-dry sheets, less 482 that UNOSAT shows at least 1% flooded, 257 of them over 25%); treated sheets lie in 21 clusters, 18 of which also hold controls. UNOSAT sample: 579 controls. Outside Chiang Rai–Phayao only radar is available, so flash-flood contamination of controls there remains and biases towards zero.
- Sensitivity, if district disaster-declaration lists for 2024 are obtained from public DDPM sources: drop controls in declared districts (administrative cuts may follow declarations rather than flooding), and, where the lists name subdistricts or villages, controls in declared tambons (a village declares its tambon). Declarations dated 1 July–31 December 2024 count. Each sheet's district is the district of its own province holding the largest share of its box in OCHA COD-AB (Royal Thai Survey Department; areas in UTM 47N), and its tambon the tambon of that district holding the largest share; a sheet whose box misses every unit (boundary offsets) takes the nearest one (`build_sheet_admin.py`). A province left without controls leaves that estimate.
- Centre at least 5 km from every treated sheet centre (2 km and 10 km as sensitivities). Distance is great-circle distance between bounding-box centres (`geo.py`; round 13g: before it, each sheet's east–west scale used its own latitude, which put 48 of the then 1,897 controls within 5 km of a treated centre).
- Height above the valley floor (sheet mean elevation minus the 5th percentile of sheet elevations in the same 1:50,000 sheet; GLO-90 for the radar sample, GLO-30 for UNOSAT) at most the 90th percentile of all treated sheets in the sample (pooled across provinces). All dry controls as a robustness check.

## 6. Inference

- Primary (radar samples): randomisation inference over 1,000 placebo footprints, each the treated footprint shifted within its province onto sheets under 1% flooded whose height above the valley floor lies within the treated range; two-sided p-value = (1 + number of placebo estimates at least as large in absolute value) / 1,001. The 5 km buffer and height cap are not re-applied around placebo sheets. If any province has fewer than twice as many eligible controls as treated sheets (the UNOSAT sample), footprints are not drawn and the wild bootstrap p-value is primary. Shift: in each draw and province a random treated sheet is placed on a random eligible control, the whole footprint moves by that vector (in km on a plane with one reference latitude per province; `geo.py`), and each shifted sheet (in random order) takes the nearest unused eligible control; real treated sheets are dropped and the weights re-estimated for the placebo group.
- Check: wild cluster restricted bootstrap (null imposed; Rademacher, 9,999 draws; CR1 t-statistic; two-sided), clusters = 1:50,000 sheets. It also tests the H1a interaction and H0: β = −0.05. If the placebo and bootstrap p-values disagree at 5%, both are reported and the placebo p-value governs.
- **Amendment proposed 2026-11-04, before registration (author to confirm).** The extended Monte Carlo (§7) finds that the placebo-footprint test rejects a true null 19.5% of the time on synthetic next rounds while the wild cluster bootstrap rejects 5.5%. The cause (`rq1_placebo_diagnostic.py`, `rq1_placebo_diagnostic.csv`, three synthetic null rounds × 200 footprints): the placebo estimates have a standard deviation of 0.0085–0.0104, about 61% of the sampling SD of the actual estimate under the null (0.0152), because shifted footprints rest on about 500 effective controls after balancing against 98 for the real treated sheets (they cover the same number of clusters, 20.7 against 21). The real footprint lies at the edge of the controls' support on height and land cover; the shifted ones lie in its middle, so the exchangeability the test assumes fails on precision. Two repairs were tested on the same synthetic rounds (`rq1_placebo_restricted_mc.py`, `rq1_placebo_restricted_mc.csv`; 200 replications per planted effect, 200 footprints per rule, same seeds as the extended Monte Carlo). (i) Restricting the pool to controls whose fitted propensity (logit of T on the balancing variables with province effects) is at least the 10th percentile of the treated sheets' (ladder 25/10/5, pool ≥ 2× treated; strict variant from the median, ≥ 1.5×) does **not** work: effective controls fall to 234 and 177, but the footprints scatter over 25.5 and 27.6 clusters against 20.7, the placebo SD stays at 0.0109 and the size at 0.200 and 0.200. (ii) Studentising the comparison, ranking |β̂/se| instead of |β̂| (Chung and Romano 2013), with the registered pool: size 0.055 on the same replications on which the bootstrap's is 0.070, power 0.56 against the bootstrap's 0.59 (SD of t across replications 1.25, across placebo draws 1.22). Proposed change: for the radar samples the wild cluster restricted bootstrap becomes the primary p-value; the placebo-footprint check is kept with the registered pool but studentised (p = (1 + number of placebo |β̂/se| at least as large as the actual |β̂/se|) / 1,001), reported beside the bootstrap and not governing; the Holm family in §3 then consists of two bootstrap p-values; the unstudentised placebo p-value and the restricted pools are dropped. Until the author confirms, the bullets above state the draft rule and this bullet the proposed one. Code: `placebo_assignment(d, rng, elig=None, min_ratio=2.0)` gained the two optional arguments for the test; the registered call is unchanged.
- "Rules out a fall of 0.05" means β̂ > −0.05 and the bootstrap rejects H0: β = −0.05 at 5% (`wcb_p_fall_005`); this replaces a separate equivalence test. The 95% interval reported is β̂ ± 1.96 × CR1 SE, labelled as understated with few treated clusters.

## 7. Power (assumptions: SD of changes 0.10 or 0.20; ICC 0.2)

| Sample | MDE, SD 0.10 | MDE, SD 0.20 | Carry-over MDD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radar, 5 km, dry in every source | 0.047 | 0.094 | 23 pp |
| Radar, reweighted (pre-registered weights) | 0.053 | — | — |
| UNOSAT, 5 km, dry in every source | 0.065 | 0.129 | 32 pp |

Bases: the 0.047, 0.065 and carry-over rows are unweighted (`rq1_2024_mde_changes.csv`, `rq1_2024_pooled_mde.csv`); the reweighted row uses the registered weights (`rq1_round_change_balance.csv`; UNOSAT reweighted 0.061, 2025 reweighted 0.030). Power and size from the 100-draw Monte Carlo (§5, §10) are for the wild bootstrap. The placebo test is simulated in the extended Monte Carlo of 2026-11-04 (`rq1_montecarlo_extended.py`, 1,000 synthetic next rounds per planted effect on the 2024 radar sample; wild cluster bootstrap with 999 draws in every replication, placebo footprints with 500 draws in the first 200 replications, the H1a interaction t-statistic and the Lee point bounds; `rq1_montecarlo_extended.csv`): its size and power for both tests are in the working paper (Table "Extended Monte Carlo") and replace the expectation stated here. Result: the wild cluster bootstrap rejects a true null 5.5% of the time (1,000 replications) and has power 0.60 against a planted −0.05 per land unit (expected sheet effect −0.035; mean estimate −0.0350, SD 0.0155, mean CR1 SE 0.0127); the placebo-footprint test rejects a true null 19.5% of the time (200 replications; Monte Carlo SE 1.5 points) and its 0.80 "power" is therefore not comparable; studentised, its size is 0.055 and its power 0.56. The reason, the repairs tested and the proposed amendment are in §6. The analytic power of the registered MDE against a fully recognised 4.6% discount (expected sheet effect −0.032 after 30% carry-over) is 0.39, against half recognition 0.13, and against a 10% realised-damage fall 0.96 (`paper_numbers.py`, table `powerLambda`); the 100-draw Monte Carlo's 63% at a planted −0.05 is higher because the synthetic SD of estimates (0.016) is below the formula's standard error. The smallest fall the radar design can rule out with 80% power at a true zero is 0.049 (SD 0.10). Treated clusters: radar 21, UNOSAT 18; these, not the control count, bind cluster inference. The MDE (about 0.05) is close to the assumed full hazard discount (4.6%, inland 100-year floodplains, Beltrán et al. 2018; 2.3–9.2% halved and doubled), so the test can detect damage-scale falls or full recognition but cannot separate partial from no recognition. The SD and ICC are assumptions until an earlier round arrives; if one does before registration, they are recalibrated on its change to 2023–26 for the same sheets and this table is replaced.

**What each sample can rule out (λ_T).** An interval excluding falls of 0.05 log points (4.9%) rules out λ_T above about 1.1 with the central 4.6% discount (0.05 / 0.046; arithmetic), and above about 0.5 only under the doubled 9.2% discount; under the halved 2.3% discount it rules out nothing in [0, 1]. Pooling the 2024 radar and 2025 samples (stacked by event, same rules) is a secondary test with more power; it is reported alongside, not substituted.

## 8. Checks on the radar map

- Seasonal paddy: rerun with a 2021–23 reference (water in no scene of any reference season) and with WorldCover cropland pixels excluded (cells of the 0.001° radar grid at least half cropland). Built before registration (`rq1_sensitivity_samples.csv`), with controls still required to be dry on the unmasked maps: the 2021–23 reference keeps 162 treated sheets (160 of the primary 198, plus 2 that cross 25% once 2021–22 water leaves the valid count) and 1,888 of the 1,891 registered controls (3 lie within 5 km of a newly treated sheet); excluding cropland keeps 32 treated sheets in 10 clusters (28 of the 198, plus 4) and all 1,891 controls. Most radar-treated sheets are mostly cropland: 70 of the 102 sheets whose non-cropland share still exceeds 25% keep less than half their box once cropland cells are removed. The cropland variant therefore has little power, and that share is itself reported.
- Before registration: radar-treated and control sheets in all four provinces checked against Sentinel-2 L2A scene classification from the flood window (`validate_s1_s2.py`: 15 Aug–31 Oct 2024 against 2023; a scene counts for a sheet if at least half its pixels are clear; optically flooded if the largest 2024 water share exceeds the largest 2023 share by more than 0.10). Optical precision (treated sheets) and contamination (controls) are reported by province. The radar rule is not changed by the check. Decision rule (author's choice, 2026-11-02): a province whose optical precision, among treated sheets observed in both years, is below one half is dropped from the primary sample and kept as a sensitivity. Because optical water misses water under crops and among buildings, precision is a lower bound; provinces with fewer than 10 observed treated sheets are not dropped but flagged.
- **Result (run 2026-11-02 and rerun 2026-11-03 on the round-13g sample, before registration; `s1_s2_validation.csv`).** 822 Sentinel-2 L2A scenes; every sample sheet observed in both years (median 8 usable scenes in 2023, 6 in 2024; no failed reads). Optical precision among treated sheets: Chiang Rai 0.57 (88 sheets), Phayao 0.64 (25), Nong Khai 0.78 (60), Nakhon Phanom 0.88 (25); pooled 0.68, median new water 0.25. Control contamination: 0.013 pooled (Nong Khai 0.054, 18 of 333; others ≤ 0.006). No province is below one half, so all four stay in the primary sample. Not pre-specified, reported for transparency: with a 0.05 new-water threshold precision is 0.59–0.92 and contamination up to 0.11 (Nong Khai); with 0.25, 0.35–0.64 (Chiang Rai 0.35), consistent with radar picking up shallower or shorter inundation in Chiang Rai. Sensitivity added: drop the 18 optically flooded Nong Khai controls (and any optically flooded control elsewhere; 24 in all).
- After registration: 100 random sheets, stratified by scale, against post-flood high-resolution imagery.
- Agreement with UNOSAT (precision 0.79, recall 0.07, kappa 0.11 in Chiang Rai–Phayao) reported as is; the rule is not tuned on UNOSAT.

## 9. Timing contingencies (decision tree)

Let R1 be the first schedule published after the 2023–26 round, and R2 the one after it.

| Case | R1 | Primary test | Persistence test | What can be concluded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) R1 on time (by 2 Dec 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027), surveyed after the floods | 2027–30 | R1 vs 2023–26 | R2 (by Dec 2030; Year 3 of the programme) | damage, administrative relief, recognised risk (briefly flooded land, persistence) |
| (b) R1 on time, surveyed before the floods | 2027–30 | R2 vs 2023–26; R1 is a placebo | none within the programme | damage, administrative relief; recognised risk only from briefly flooded (UNOSAT-only) land |
| (c) one-year extension under Act s. 19 para. 3, surveyed after the floods | 2028–31 (published by early Dec 2027) | R1 | R2 (by Dec 2031; Year 4) | as (a), persistence late |
| (b)+(c) extension, surveyed before the floods | 2028–31 | R2 (by Dec 2031; Year 4) | none | as (b); the 2025-flood sample carries Chapter 2 until then |

- Survey periods are taken from each provincial committee's published schedule or written reply; if unknown at R1's release, case (a) is assumed and case (b) reported as a sensitivity. The case is set province by province; the primary test uses every sample province in case (a) or (c), and provinces in case (b) move to the R1 placebo. The analysis waits until every sample province's R1 is published or one year has passed since the first, whichever is sooner. Holm (§3) is then computed once for the R1 family, over the R1 runs recorded by that date; the case-(b) provinces' R2 runs form the R2 family, adjusted separately once the last of them is recorded (the same waiting rule applied to R2); a province published later is reported in an update, outside the confirmatory family. Each run's `--post` folder holds the R1 province files copied unchanged from the dated snapshot in which each first appears (`download_treasury_appraisal.py`; with `--package` if R1 is a new data.go.th package), and the sensitivities and Lee bounds run on the same province subsets (`--provinces`).
- Treasury offices were briefed on an extension on 28 Oct 2026; Chachoengsao's committee considered extending its land, other-title and building schedules on 2 Nov 2026; on 3 Nov 2026 the Chainat, Phatthalung and Trat committees considered the same, Chainat stating one year, to 31 Dec 2027 (no decision, central approval or Gazette notice seen; as of 2026-11-03; `04_analysis/treasury_2027_round_status.md`). Case (c) is therefore the likeliest case, but the case is still set province by province from the published schedules. Registering before any publication is unaffected by the case. Added 2026-11-04: whether provinces holding treated sheets extend their schedules more often than other provinces is recorded as a descriptive outcome (an extension is itself a possible administrative response to the floods), with no confirmatory status; the 2026 floods in Nakhon Phanom enter the same R1 change under case (c), and the 2026-flood drop of §5 is the sensitivity for that.
- If an earlier round arrives, add a pre-trend test on its change to 2023–26 for the same sheets.
- Later floods. Nakhon Phanom's flooded sheets flooded again in August–October 2026 (18 of 25 above 25% by the registered radar rule; the Governor declared Mueang Nakhon Phanom an emergency disaster zone in August 2026): if its R1 survey postdates them, its estimate mixes two floods. The primary sample is not changed (re-flooding is an outcome of the same hazard, so conditioning on it would select on the outcome's cause); the 2026-flood drop of §5 is reported beside it, and the case of §9 is set by the 2024 floods. Disclosures: the rule and the choice to keep it a sensitivity were both fixed on 2026-11-03, after the 2026 shares, including the 18 of 25, had been computed; 2026 has more radar coverage than the 2023 reference (Sentinel-1C and 1D: 228 scenes on 65 dates, against 174 on 42), so the any-scene rule flags 2026 water more readily and the count dropped more likely errs high than low; the check that the re-read 2023 reference reproduces the primary map runs one way (primary-valid pixels must be reference-valid; pixels valid only in the re-read are not tested, and could only add to the sheets dropped); and the drop covers the 2024 radar sample only, not the UNOSAT or 2025 samples, whose 2026 shares are not computed.
- The 2025 floods (§10) follow the same tree with their own survey timing; they are more likely to postdate R1's surveys, so for them case (b) is the default expectation.

## 11. Interpretation rules

- These rules use secondary contrasts and are interpretation, not confirmatory tests. Falls graded by flood duration (number of 2024 radar scenes with water on the sheet, to be computed from `flood_s1_grd.py` outputs before R1 is read): damage. Uniform cuts across a flooded area: administrative relief. Falls persisting into R2 (case (a) only), or reaching briefly flooded (UNOSAT-only) land: recognised risk. The duration contrast is made within Chiang Rai and Phayao (sheets flooded in both maps vs UNOSAT only), so it is not confounded with province.
- A null H1a interaction says nothing about ex-ante capitalisation.
- An interval excluding falls of 0.05 rules out responses as large as New York's after Sandy (about 0.10), not as small as English prices (3–4%). Official values move in zone steps, so small responses may appear only in carry-over.
